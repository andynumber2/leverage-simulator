<objective>
Reduce the D-24 `solveIrr` contribution-schedule branch's cost by doing less work per solve, then
re-measure it at a sample count that carries a verdict rather than an anecdote, and resolve it
either way.

This branch is the larger of Phase 7's two overruns: 7303.90ms raw, 8961.84ms normalized against
the same 1000ms budget on D-17 baseline run 32654061079, roughly 8.96x over and roughly 7.67x worse
than that run's own headline figure. It was recorded at `sampleCount=1` and left as an
informational row. ROADMAP criterion 4 says it must not be left that way a second time.

07.1-RESEARCH.md is explicit that pool tuning alone cannot close a 8.96x gap: a three-worker,
kernel-bound branch has at best 3x of parallel headroom and that headroom is already spent. So this
plan does not try to close it with the pool. It attacks the per-solve cost directly, with levers
that need no new Key Decision, and it reaches ROADMAP criterion 4's escalation path deliberately if
those levers are not enough.

**The tension criterion 4 and criterion 5 create, resolved here rather than left for two tasks to
fight over.** Criterion 4 wants a larger sample count on this arm. Criterion 5 wants the whole bench
suite back under 30,000ms on a baseline host where this arm alone currently costs 7,304ms of a
43,259ms run. Those pull in opposite directions. The resolution is a hard ceiling stated as a task
acceptance criterion: this arm's total contribution to bench runtime on the baseline must not exceed
the 7,304ms it already contributes. The sample count is chosen against the measured per-sweep cost
to fit inside that ceiling, and the chosen N is disclosed in the info line. A cheaper solve buys a
better sample count; it never buys a longer suite.

Purpose: make the `solveIrr` branch's cost describe less work, not a shorter measurement.
Output: a cheaper bisection, a re-measured arm with a real sample count, and a resolved disposition.
</objective>

width falls at or below a new named constant, and return the bracket mid-point. Name the constant
for what it bounds (the rate, not the NPV), set it to 1e-9, and document in the constants block that
