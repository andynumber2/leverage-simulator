### `SeamRecord` gains one field

`SeamRecord` currently carries `kind`, `firstDate`, `lastDate`, `sourceBefore`, `sourceAfter` and `method`. This plan adds one required boolean:

`degradesToNonDaily: boolean` is `true` when the seam replaces a genuinely-daily input with one derived from a lower-frequency source, and `false` otherwise. A monthly-to-daily interpolation is `true`. A splice whose `sourceBefore` is a monthly series is `true`. A one-day carry-forward inside a daily source is `false`, because a bond-market holiday does not make the rate series stop being daily. This boolean is what `computeTierRanges` scans; matching on the free-text `method` string would make the tier boundary depend on prose.

D-16 rates the seam record `costly` to change because Phase 5's on-screen provenance renders from it. Adding this field inside the same phase that introduced the record is the cheap moment; do not add a second field later without the same deliberation.

### Rate source precedence (`RATE_SOURCE_PRECEDENCE`)

Highest precedence first. Each source owns its entire available range; a lower-precedence source is used strictly before the first date of every higher-precedence source, never interleaved with one.

| Rank | Raw stem | Frequency | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `RATE-DFF` | daily | The overnight funding rate, the closest published proxy for a broker's cost of carry, used from its own first date onward |
| 2 | `RATE-DTB3` | daily | The daily three-month bill rate, used for the window between its first date and the first date of `RATE-DFF` |
| 3 | `RATE-TB3MS` | monthly | The monthly three-month bill rate, interpolated to daily, used before `RATE-DTB3` begins |
| 4 | `RATE-NBER` | monthly | The NBER Macrohistory short rate mirrored on FRED, interpolated to daily, used before `RATE-TB3MS` begins |

Every boundary date is read from the fetched data, never hardcoded. That is what resolves the overlap RESEARCH.md's Pitfall 4 names: `RATE-NBER` runs to March 1934 and `RATE-TB3MS` begins in January 1934, a full quarter of overlap with no single hand-off date. Under this rule `RATE-TB3MS` wins its entire range and `RATE-NBER` is used strictly before `RATE-TB3MS`'s first date, which is a decision recorded as one splice seam rather than an implicit first-source-found resolution.

Instruments are never mixed inside one window. Rank 1 and rank 2 are different instruments, so a gap in the daily source is handled by plan 02-02's carry-forward policy within that source, not by reaching down a rank for a substitute value.

### Monthly-to-daily interpolation

`interpolateMonthlyToDaily(monthlyRows, calendar, firstIndex, lastIndex)` produces one value per calendar date in the range by linear interpolation between the two bracketing monthly anchors. A monthly observation's anchor date is the first day of its month, which is how plan 02-03 wrote it. Dates before the first anchor or after the last are not extrapolated: the interpolated run is bounded by the anchors. Every contiguous interpolated run produces exactly one `interpolation` seam with `degradesToNonDaily` set true.

### Total-return construction (D-15, annotated 2026-08-17)

**This construction path applies to the S&P alone.** Every other symbol's total return is sourced across its whole life and reaches the compiler as a raw series with no constructed segment: the nine funds get theirs reconstructed from their own dividend events in plan 02-06, and the Nasdaq-100 gets its own total-return index in plan 02-07. The S&P is the one symbol where construction is unavoidable, because an index pays no dividends, so its vendor-adjusted column equals its close on all 24,773 bars and carries no dividend information at all. Its real daily total-return series begins 1988-01-04, exactly the date D-14 declared before the series was named.

Implement the construction generically over `(price series, real total-return series, monthly yield series)` rather than special-casing a scope name, and let it apply where a monthly yield input exists for that scope. Only the S&P has one, so only the S&P gets a constructed run, without a scope literal appearing anywhere in the compiler.

For a scope whose real daily total-return series begins at date `S`:

- On and after `S`, the total-return series is the real daily series, stored exactly as sourced.
- Before `S`, the level is chained backward from `S` using the daily price return plus a dividend contribution, so the joined series is continuous at `S` with no jump and no duplicated bar.
- The dividend contribution for a bar is the interpolated annualized yield for that date, multiplied by the number of calendar days elapsed since the previous trading day, divided by 365. Calendar-day accrual matches SIM-03's convention, so a three-day weekend accrues three days of dividend rather than one trading day's worth. State this convention in the module doc comment so Phase 3 inherits it rather than re-deriving it.
- Two seams are recorded: one `interpolation` seam over the constructed run with `degradesToNonDaily` true, and one `splice` seam at `S` naming the constructed source before and the real daily source after, with `degradesToNonDaily` true.

### `computeTierRanges` contract (D-14, D-05)

`computeTierRanges(pairSeams, rateSeams, pairRange, rateRange)` returns `TierRanges` as `{ strict: DateRange | null, extended: DateRange | null }`.

- `extended` is the intersection of the pair's own date range with the rate series' date range. When the intersection is empty, `extended` is `null`.
- `strict` is `extended` narrowed at the front past every seam with `degradesToNonDaily` true on either the pair's own series or the rate series: `strict.firstDate` is the day after the latest such seam's `lastDate`, or `extended.firstDate` when no such seam overlaps. `strict.lastDate` equals `extended.lastDate`.
- When the narrowed start would fall after `extended.lastDate`, `strict` is `null`, never a zero-length or inverted range.

The pair is `(scope, dividend mode)` where dividend mode is `price-return` or `total-return`, which is exactly the promoted `SeriesKey` from plan 02-01. Nothing in the codebase declares a tier boundary as a literal date.
