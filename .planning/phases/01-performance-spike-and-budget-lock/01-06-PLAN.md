## Known Limitations (recorded honestly, not deferred)

1. **The recorded figures change, and PROJECT.md is deliberately not updated.** Once PERF-02 and PERF-05 are amortized, their normalized figures will differ from the values `01-SPIKE-RESULTS.md` section 2 records and from the values the two PROJECT.md Key Decisions rows cite. Per the operator's locked gap-closure decision, this plan does not edit those PROJECT.md rows and does not re-litigate either architecture decision. `01-VERIFICATION.md` independently assessed both decisions against these gaps and found both well supported: the plain-JS decision rests primarily on PERF-03's fully resolved 327.40ms figure, and the Canvas decision rests on an ordering argument against a fully resolved 4.41ms loser. Section 6 of `01-SPIKE-RESULTS.md` records the resolved numbers and states explicitly that the PROJECT.md rows were left as they are and why, so a later reader finds the pointer rather than a silent discrepancy.

2. **A batched per-call figure is not identical in kind to a cold single-call figure.** Running a workload 500 times inside one timed span lets the JIT reach a steadier tier than a single cold call would. This is the same tradeoff the throwaway Rust spike already accepted and disclosed, and it is strictly better than reporting a number at or below the timer's resolution. The batch size is recorded next to every figure it produced so the tradeoff is visible rather than implied.

## Prohibition Recall Note

No canon security or compliance candidate (OWASP, prototype pollution, path traversal, injection, GDPR, generic fairness) survived stage 2 for this plan, so nothing was dropped under the canon-referral rule. The two kept prohibitions are project-values items about not weakening the instrument and not laundering an amortized figure as a single-call one. <objective>
Make every recorded measurement clear the timer-resolution floor the harness already declares, and make the harness fail loudly instead of silently when it cannot.

`MIN_MEASUREMENT_MS = 10` is declared in `bench/calibration.ts` and documented as a hard requirement, and no code anywhere compares a measured value against it. This is not theoretical: `01-SPIKE-RESULTS.md` records PERF-02's raw minimum-of-five at `0.09999999962747097ms` and PERF-05's winning arm at a literal `0ms`, both far under the floor, both fed through `checkBudget` and recorded as pass with no diagnostic anywhere. The same root cause reaches `calibrationScore` and `normalize` from a different call site, where a zero score would silently produce `Infinity` for every downstream figure.

Enforcement alone would turn the suite red, because the current measurements violate the floor. That is the point of the gap, and it is why enforcement and amortization land in the same slice.

Purpose: a harness that cannot tell "this is fast" apart from "the clock could not see it" produces numbers no later phase can lock a budget against.
Output: an enforced floor, a batched amortization helper, three amortized call sites, guarded calibration, a bounded and diagnostic worker-failure path, and a spike-results addendum recording the resolved figures.
</objective>
